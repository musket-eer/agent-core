**Title: AI Fundamentals – Beginner-Level Lecture Notes**  
**Objective:** Introduce core AI concepts for undergraduate students with no prior background.  

---

### **1. What is Artificial Intelligence (AI)?**  
**Definition:** AI refers to machines designed to perform tasks that typically require human intelligence, such as reasoning, learning, decision-making, or perception.  

**Key Ideas:**  
- **Narrow AI:** Specialized in one task (e.g., chatbots, recommendation systems).  
- **General AI (hypothetical):** Human-like adaptability across diverse tasks (not yet achieved).  

---

### **2. Core Concepts**  
#### **a) Machine Learning (ML)**  
- **Definition:** A subset of AI where systems learn from data without explicit programming.  
- **Example:** Spam filters improve by analyzing labeled emails ("spam" vs. "not spam").  

#### **b) Neural Networks & Deep Learning**  
- **Neural Network:** A model inspired by the human brain’s interconnected neurons.  
- **Deep Learning:** Uses multi-layered neural networks for complex tasks (e.g., image recognition).  

#### **c) Natural Language Processing (NLP)**  
- Enables machines to understand/generate human language (e.g., ChatGPT, translation tools).  

---

### **3. How AI Systems Learn**  
- **Supervised Learning:** Trained on labeled data (input-output pairs).  
- **Unsupervised Learning:** Finds patterns in unlabeled data (e.g., customer segmentation).  
- **Reinforcement Learning:** Learns via rewards/punishments (e.g., game-playing AI).  

---

### **4. Ethical Considerations**  
- **Bias:** AI can inherit biases from training data (e.g., discriminatory hiring algorithms).  
- **Transparency:** "Black box" problems make some AI decisions hard to explain.  

---

### **5. Real-World Applications**  
- **Healthcare:** Diagnosing diseases from medical scans.  
- **Autonomous Vehicles:** Self-driving cars use AI for navigation.  
- **Finance:** Fraud detection algorithms.
